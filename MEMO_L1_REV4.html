Claim. ProSeCo's corrector fires every step, but a cheap learned probe reading the shape of the predictor's confidence distribution (min, std, entropy over active positions — not just the mean) predicts corrector-usefulness with a margin scalar-confidence controllers (CadLLM, AdaBlock-dLLM, DepCap, Dynamic-dLLM, KLASS, Apple-RL, TraceLock, LESS) cannot match, and using it as a live skip policy trends toward matched-compute accuracy wins on both GSM8K and HumanEval. This is a paper about extending ProSeCo, not competing with it.

Where the evidence is. The step-level predictive signal is certified: on 23,120 corrector invocations from ProSeCo LLaDA-SFT, held out by prompt, L1's shape features beat the scalar-confidence baseline by ΔAUC = +0.019 GSM8K / +0.033 HumanEval, 95% cluster-bootstrap CI lower bounds above zero on both, P(moat ≤ 0) = 0.000. The task-level accuracy signal is trending: in a small pilot (n=40 per benchmark) L1 was the top-accuracy policy on both benchmarks, but the margin was one sample per benchmark and CIs straddle zero at that N. That was the expected outcome of a deliberately underpowered pilot with a pre-registered success criterion, not a disappointing result.

What I'm asking for. The pre-registered decisive run needs ~6× the statistical power of the pilot (100+ prompts × 3–5 seeds, CadLLM retuned to matched NFE): 3+ weeks on my T4, 2–3 days on Cornell A100s. Running it on your cluster simultaneously kills the int8/T4 quantization confound the T4 setup imposes on my logit-shape signal. The decisive experiment is fully specified and pre-committed; the compute is the only missing piece. That's a normal collaboration inflection point — after de-risk, before headline — and it's why the ask is real.

Section 1

The idea, precisely

ProSeCo's corrector runs at every step where the fixed schedule fires (ω=2 for both GSM8K and HumanEval, per Table 3 Max), regardless of whether the sequence-state at that step actually needs correction. Empirically, most invocations are wasted — the corrector agrees with the predictor's argmax on the first inner iteration and adds nothing.

L1 is a small learned probe (5 features → 16 → 1 MLP, ~100 params, one matmul per invocation opportunity) that predicts whether the corrector will do anything and skips it when the prediction is no. The features are the shape of the predictor's per-position top-1-probability distribution over active-mask positions: mean, min, std, plus entropy_mean and entropy_max. Position features (block index, active-mask size) are deliberately excluded — Phase A showed they add only ~+0.001 AUC on top of confidence shape and inflate overfitting risk on small eval subsets.

Why "shape, not mean" is the paper's non-trivial claim. Existing training-free adaptive schedulers (CadLLM, AdaBlock-dLLM, DepCap, Dynamic-dLLM, KLASS) and lightweight learned ones (Apple-RL, TraceLock, LESS) all key on a scalar — usually mean confidence, sometimes a KL-derived stability index. Two sequences with identical mean confidence can have wildly different distributions: one where every token is moderately certain, one where most tokens are certain but two are garbage. Only the second needs the corrector. Mean is blind to that difference. Shape isn't.

The two-phase proof structure:

  • Phase A (done): does the shape signal actually predict corrector behavior? Answered yes with a clean statistical margin. Section 2.
  • Phase B (in progress): does using the signal as a live skip policy improve downstream accuracy at matched compute? Pilot ran; decisive experiment needs A100s. Section 3.

The gap exists because prediction is correlation. "The corrector would have made an edit" ≠ "that edit would have fixed the final answer." A big predictive edge at the step level dilutes into a small accuracy edge at the task level. That's not a flaw — it's the physics of the measurement.

Section 2 · Phase A

The signal is real, certified on 23k held-out invocations

S1 v3 protocol: ran ProSeCo LLaDA-SFT (int8 on a T4) on 100 GSM8K + 100 HumanEval prompts (200 prompts, 23,120 corrector invocations), logged one record per invocation with the label was_useful = "corrector argmax disagreed with predictor argmax on the first inner iteration." Paper thesis

A learned policy over confidence-distribution-shape features (not just mean) can predict, per corrector opportunity, whether ProSeCo's corrector will do anything — and can skip it when it won't. On 23,120 held-out invocations from ProSeCo LLaDA-SFT, that signal beats the scalar-confidence-linear controller family (CadLLM et al.) by tight, statistically airtight margins on both GSM8K and HumanEval. The paper is about turning that predictive edge into a matched-compute accuracy edge inside ProSeCo — extending the framework, not competing with it.

L1 was the top-accuracy policy on both benchmarks. +5.0 pp over fixed on GSM8K, +2.5 pp over the best cadllm_linear on GSM8K, +2.5 pp over both on HumanEval. Directionally clean. But the margins are 1 sample per benchmark, so with n=40 the paired-bootstrap 95% CI on the primary comparison (L1 vs NFE-matched CadLLM) is [+0.000, +0.075], P(Δ ≤ 0) = 0.35 on both benchmarks — the CI just touches zero, doesn't clear it. Per the pre-registered criterion this is the ambiguous zone, not a pass, not a kill. Kill trigger (L1 loses to fixed) was not fired.

Matched-compute correction, honestly. Read the NFE column: l1_mlp:0.40's advantage over the best cadllm_linear comes partly from spending 13-25 % more compute (343 vs 304, 449 vs 360). At the actually-matched pair (l1_mlp:0.60 at 323 NFE vs cadllm_linear:0.25 at 334 NFE, both on GSM8K), accuracy is identical (0.675). The pilot's cleanest single-comparison-story is L1 buys +5.0 pp over fixed at essentially matched NFE (343 vs 344) on GSM8K. Everything else is either underpowered or off-Pareto. The n=100+, retuned-CadLLM run below fixes the comparison.

Pre-committed success criteria — locked before any results seen

PASS: paired cluster-bootstrap 95 % CI on the L1 vs NFE-matched-CadLLM accuracy delta has lower bound above zero on GSM8K OR HumanEval. Written up as an accuracy table, the pitch converts to a submission plan.
AMBIGUOUS: CI straddles zero on both. Weakens the accuracy claim but is not fatal — see paper floor below.
KILL: L1 loses to fixed (not just to CadLLM) on both benchmarks with paired CI upper bound below zero. Learned policy is worse than doing nothing; L1 dies for real.
Third outcome (added 2026-08-22): if the A100 run also lands ambiguous, the accuracy-improvement claim is dead at this model scale. Paper pivots to (a) diagnostic-signal contribution (the certified Phase A result — shape > mean on 23k held-out) + (b) compute-savings-at-parity contribution.

Section 5 · Paper floor + open concerns

What the paper looks like in each outcome, honestly

If decisive run PASSES

Main-conference target (NeurIPS 2027). Contribution: "ProSeCo + learned skip = same quality at ~X % less compute, or +Y pp accuracy at matched compute, driven by a signal every prior adaptive scheduler is blind to." Ablations, MBPP, MATH, bigger model scan — all trivial extensions with A100 hours.

If decisive run is AMBIGUOUS or floors

Workshop paper. Contribution splits into two: (1) the certified Phase A diagnostic — predictor confidence distribution shape beats mean-confidence for corrector-usefulness prediction on 23k held-out invocations, useful for anyone building adaptive samplers on masked diffusion LMs; (2) L1 as a compute-parity method — matches CadLLM's accuracy while using strictly fewer or equal FLOPs, driven by better skip decisions on the invocations where correctors don't help.

The paper has a floor either way. What A100 time decides is main-conference vs. workshop, not whether there's a paper.

Open concerns, spelled out because Yair will ask

  • Label validity. Phase A's was_useful label = corrector disagreement. Disagreement is mechanically related to how peaked the predictor's distribution is, so shape features predicting disagreement is expected. Disagreement ≠ improvement. Phase B's downstream-accuracy endpoint is what actually tests whether the signal is exploitable, per benchmark.
  • int8 / T4 confound. All step-level and pilot data collected under 8-bit bitsandbytes on Tesla T4. Quantization distorts logit magnitudes; L1's signal is the logit distribution's shape. Whether the moat survives at bf16 is unknown. The A100 run resolves this simultaneously with the statistical-power question — that's what makes the combined ask compelling.
  • CadLLM-as-proxy. Everything here compares against a linear-in-scalar-confidence proxy of the CadLLM mechanism family (verified from their paper), not their released code. A head-to-head with the actual implementation is a natural Phase B+ item once compute is available.
  • Prompt template deviation. GSM8K eval uses no few-shot examples (paper's canonical eval uses nemo_skills 8-shot CoT). This affects absolute accuracy but not the L1-vs-CadLLM comparison at matched compute (both policies see the same prompts). Absolute accuracy in the table above is lower than the paper's Table 3 numbers for exactly this reason.

Primary metric: ROC-AUC on held-out invocations (right lens for a binary label; not R²)
Secondary metrics: Average Precision (AP) and coefficient of determination (R², reported only for continuity with prior drafts)
Uncertainty: 500-iter cluster bootstrap over held-out samples (not invocations), fixed models fit on the training split, resampled predictions evaluated on each bootstrap draw
P(moat ≤ 0): fraction of bootstrap resamples where ΔAUC (or ΔAP, ΔR²) is ≤ 0
